Chapter 1

INTRODUCTION


1.1   Research Background and Motivation

Networked information systems support communication and services across organi-
sational and public environments. Their operation depends on the exchange of traffic
through infrastructures whose activity must be monitored to identify policy viola-
tions and potentially harmful behavior. An intrusion detection system (IDS)analyses
observationsfromahostornetworkandreportsactivitythatmayrequirefurtherinves-
tigation. Network IDSs commonly analyse packets or derived flow records, allowing
traffic to be examined through attributes such as duration, protocol, and aggregated
communication behavior.
   Two broad detection approaches are signature-based and anomaly-based.    A
signature-based detector compares observed activity with descriptions of previously
identifiedthreats;thiscansupportrecognitionofknownpatterns,butitscoveragede-
pends on the available signatures and their maintenance. An anomaly-based detector
models expected behavior and flags departures from that model. Such departures are
notnecessarilymalicious: legitimatechangesinusers,services,ornetworkconditions
may also be unusual.  Surveys of intrusion detection describe these approaches and
their associated trade-offs, including the challenge of balancing detection capability
against false alarms [1], [2].

    Machine-learning methods provide a way to estimate decision boundaries or rep-
resentations from labelled or unlabelled traffic rather than relying exclusively on
manually specified rules.   Their use does not remove the need for careful evalua-
tion. Resultscandependonfeatureconstruction,labelquality,classdistribution,and
how data are partitioned. CICIDS2017 provides labelled network traffic intended for
intrusion-detection research and supports binary as well as attack-category experi-
ments [3], [2]. However, benchmark evidence must be interpreted in relation to how
thedatasetwasgeneratedandprepared;lateranalysishasdocumentedevaluationand
data-quality considerations relevant to CICIDS2017 [4].
    This thesis investigates a hierarchical approach in which a first classifier dis-
tinguishes benign from attack traffic and a second classifier assigns known attack
categories. It also examines whether an Autoencoder-derived representation changes
performance relative to the original flow features, and whether the Stage-1 attack
probability contributes useful information to Stage 2. Prior studies have investigated
Autoencoder-basedandtwo-stageintrusion-detectionsystems,buttheirresultsdonot
establish that the representation or meta-feature proposed here will be beneficial [5],
[6].  Finally, because a closed-set classifier is trained with a fixed set of labels, the
study evaluates experimentally held-out attack categories using confidence-based re-
jection,One-ClassSVM,andIsolationForest. Previousworkonopen-setrecognition
andhybridIDSmotivatesthisevaluation,butdoesnotestablishitseffectivenesshere
[7], [8].  This is a controlled unknown-category evaluation, not a claim of detecting
real-world zero-day exploits.

1.2   Problem Statement

Networkflowrecordscancontainmanyattributeswithdifferentscales,distributions,
and relationships to attack labels.  It is therefore not established in advance whether
the original representation is the most suitable input for the proposed classifiers or

whetheranAutoencoder-derivedlatentrepresentationchangespredictiveperformance
and computational cost.  The first problem is to compare these representations un-
der a controlled data protocol, rather than to assume that dimensionality reduction
necessarily improves detection.
    The first stage must distinguish benign traffic from attacks while limiting both
missed attacks and false alarms.  XGBoost and a multilayer perceptron (MLP) offer
different model families for this binary task, but their relative performance depends
on the data and evaluation conditions.  This study therefore treats their comparison
as empirical.  It will consider attack-sensitive and class-aware measures as well as
computationalcost;itwillnotinfersuperiorityfromthealgorithms’generalproperties
alone.
    The hierarchical design introduces a dependency between stages.   If Stage 1
classifies an attack as benign, the sample may not be passed to the Stage-2 attack
classifier. Conversely,anattackprobabilityfromStage1maysummarizeinformation

outoffoldsothattheStage-2trainingproceduredoesnotsimplylearnfromin-sample
Stage-1 predictions; this follows the general principle of stacked generalization [9].

be evaluated as candidate mechanisms, but none is assumed to separate every unseen
attack while retaining all known traffic. The relevant question is the balance between
identifying held-out categories and preserving recognition of known classes.
    Finally,validcomparisonsrequireattentiontoclassimbalance,datasetpreparation,
and information leakage. Accuracy alone can obscure performance on less frequent

1.3   Research Objectives

The general objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a hybrid two-stage
network intrusion detection framework using CICIDS2017, with particular attention
to learned representations, binary screening, known-attack classification, and experi-
mentally unseen attack categories.
    To  meet  this  objective,  the  study  aims  to  establish  a  reproducible,  leakage-
controlleddata-preparationprocedureandtoinvestigateoriginalflowfeaturesagainst
Autoencoder-derived latent features.  It will compare XGBoost and MLP as Stage-1
BENIGN-versus-ATTACK classifiers and generate out-of-fold Stage-1 attack prob-
abilities.  The Stage-2 experiment will evaluate whether adding those probabilities
as a meta-feature changes known-attack multiclass performance.  Further objectives
are to examine confidence-based rejection, One-Class SVM, and Isolation Forest in
leave-one-attack-class-out experiments; assess the resulting pipeline end to end; con-
ductablationstudies;anddocumentreproducibilityandcomputationalrequirements.
Theseobjectivesdescribeintendedevaluations;theydonotassertthatanymodelhas
been trained or that any improvement has been achieved.

1.4   Research Questions

The study is organised around four research questions. They specify comparisons to
be tested and are not conclusions about expected outcomes.
RQ1:WhatistheimpactofAutoencoder-derivedlatentrepresentationcomparedwith
original flow features?
RQ2:How do XGBoost and MLP compare as Stage-1 binary intrusion classifiers?
RQ3:Does adding the Stage-1 attack probability as a meta-feature improve Stage-2
multiclass classification?
RQ4:How effectively can novelty detectors identify previously unseen attack cate-
gories without excessively rejecting known attacks?

1.5   Research Scope

The research is limited to offline, network-based intrusion detection using flow-level
records from CICIDS2017 as the primary experimental dataset.   The supervised
tasks comprise binary classification of BENIGN and ATTACK traffic and multiclass
classificationamongattackcategoriestreatedasknownduringtraining. Theproposed
comparisonsincludeoriginalandAutoencoder-derivedrepresentations,XGBoostand
MLP in Stage 1, and latent-only versus probability-augmented inputs in Stage 2.
Unknown-categoryevaluationconsidersconfidence-basedrejection,One-ClassSVM,
and Isolation Forest under a leave-one-attack-class-out protocol.
   Thetermunseenattackcategoryrefersheretoalabelwithheldfrommodeltraining
for a defined experiment.  It does not establish that the withheld traffic represents a
previously unknown exploit in an operational network.  The evaluation is based on
an offline benchmark and does not, by itself, demonstrate live detection, deployment
readiness, or effectiveness against traffic outside the dataset.   The exact partition
rules,held-outcategories,thresholds,andanycalibrationproceduremustbereported

1.6   Research Methodology

The research follows an experimental machine-learning methodology supported by a
review of intrusion detection, representation learning, hierarchical classification, and
novelty detection.  The dataset is examined before modelling to establish its feature
and label structure.   Data cleaning, label construction, and partitioning are then
definedsothattransformationslearnedfromdataarefittedusingtraininginformation
only and are applied consistently to validation and test data.   The final split and
preprocessing decisions must match the verified implementation and be documented
in the methodology chapter.
    The first set of controlled experiments compares original flow attributes with
Autoencoder-derived latent features.  The second compares XGBoost and MLP for
Stage-1 binary classification.  Evaluation should report class-sensitive measures, in-
cluding attack recall, precision, F1-score, macro-F1, and false-positive rate; ROC-
AUC and PR-AUC may provide complementary threshold-independent summaries
where appropriate.   Since class prevalence affects the interpretation of precision–
recall results, metric definitions and averaging choices must be stated explicitly [10].
Training and inference cost are also recorded if supported by the execution environ-
ment and measured consistently.
    For the Stage-2 experiment, Stage-1 attack probabilities are generated out of fold
fortrainingexamples,thencomparedasanaddedmeta-featureagainstalatent-feature-
only baseline. This design is intended to reduce leakage from in-sample predictions;
it does not by itself guarantee leakage-free evaluation unless the surrounding splits
and preprocessing are also controlled.  If probability calibration is included in the
confirmed protocol, its method and calibration metrics should be reported separately

2.9.3   Probability-Based Meta-Features and Stacking

4.2.6   Dataset Preparation Limitations

Several qualifications apply when interpreting results from these data.  First, the reported

validation, or evaluation. The exact degree of imbalance must be established from the per-

record split does not ensure that temporally adjacent, host-related, or otherwise correlated

independenceoftheremaining2,096,804observations. Third,theLOACOscalerisreported

than a direct substitute for independently collected operational traffic [4]. These limitations

4.4   Evaluation Protocol and Performance Metrics

The evaluation is organized around distinct populations and prediction targets: binary BE-
NIGN/ATTACKscreening,conditionalclassificationamongknownattackcategories,attack-
only novelty scoring, and final composed system decisions.   The metrics have different
denominatorsandanswerdifferentquestions. Table4.12summarizestheimplementedeval-
uationpopulations,outputs,groundtruth,andinterpretationlimits;thefollowingsubsections
state the details needed to interpret the reported values.

4.4.1   Stage-1 Binary Evaluation

Stage 1 evaluates binary predictions for BENIGN (label 0) and ATTACK (label 1) at the
configured threshold of 0.5. Its reported metrics are accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision,
attackrecall(detectionrate),F1-score,ROC-AUC,AveragePrecision,andaconfusionmatrix.
Precision, recall, and F1 refer to the positive ATTACK class. The confusion matrix uses the
label order BENIGN=0 and ATTACK=1, with true labels on rows and predicted labels on
columns. Thepositive-classconventionmakesfalsenegativescorrespondtoattacksassigned
BENIGN and false positives correspond to benign flows assigned ATTACK.
    The metrics emphasize complementary aspects of the binary decision.  Accuracy is the
fractionofcorrectpredictionsintheevaluatedpopulation;balancedaccuracyaveragesclass-
specific recall; precision measures the fraction of predicted attacks that are truly attacks;
and attack recall measures the fraction of true attacks detected at the operating threshold.
ROC-AUCsummarizesrankingoverthresholds,whiletheJSONkeypr                            auciscalculatedby
scikit-learn’saverage      precision      score. It is therefore Average Precision, not a generic
trapezoidalnumericalintegralofaprecision–recallplot. PrecisionandAveragePrecisionare
useful alongside recall when class frequencies differ, since the precision–recall relationship
is sensitive to the prevalence of the positive class [10].  The single-threshold metrics and
threshold-independent ranking metrics should not be conflated.

4.4.3   Standalone Novelty Evaluation

Standalone novelty metrics are computed on true attack observations in the test partition.
The known-negative population comprises known attack categories; the unknown-positive
population comprises the selected held-out attack category.  BENIGN observations are ex-
cluded.   For each detector, larger novelty scores indicate stronger evidence for rejection,
and the threshold is selected from known-attack validation scores at the 95th percentile,
corresponding to a target known rejection proportion of 0.05.
    Thereportedquantitiesareunknownrecall,unknownF1,knownfalse-rejectionrate,open-
set ROC-AUC, and open-set Average Precision.  Here,known                   false    rejection      rateis
the proportion of known attack test observations whose novelty scores cross the rejection
threshold. It is not the benign false-positive rate and is not automatically the final system’s
false-rejection rate.   Unknown recall and F1 depend on the validation-selected operating
threshold. ROC-AUCandAveragePrecisioninsteadassessscorerankingoverthresholds. A
validationtargetof5%doesnotguaranteea5%rejectionrateonthetestdistribution;score-

4.5   Reproducibility and Threats to Validity

The experiments include several mechanisms that support traceability:  a fixed seed of 42,
JSON configuration files, a class-aware fixed partition, stable row hashes, stored label map-
pings, experiment identifiers, model and run metadata, training-history records, prediction
or probability artifacts, and JSON evaluation reports. Together, these records can connect a
configurationtoitsdatasplitandsavedoutputs. Theyareusefulreproducibilitycontrols,but
the presence of configuration and summary files alone does not establish exact reproduction
of every result or strict determinism across machines.

4.5.1   Internal Validity

Splitdependenceandresidualflowsimilarity.  Thefixed70/15/15partitionisclass-aware
andrandomattheobservationlevel. Thereportedremovalof3,167exactduplicatesreduces
exact row repetition, but does not establish independence among distinct flows.   Related
observationsmaysharecapturetimes,endpoints,traffic-generationconditions,orotherchar-
acteristics and may therefore occur in more than one partition.  The split is not established
to be chronological or grouped by host, session, or capture day.  A random split can still
answer a within-corpus interpolation question, but it offers weaker evidence for temporal or
environment-shift generalization than a chronological, group-aware, or independent-capture
evaluation.  This is a potential threat, not proof that the reported performance is inflated;
its magnitude would require a separate controlled split analysis. Such dependence is a rec-
ognized concern in evaluating machine-learning systems with related training and test data
[16].

Fold-level early stopping.The five-fold Stage 1 training probabilities are out-of-fold with
respect to fitting the principal model parameters: each fold model is trained using the other
four folds.   However, the held-out fold labels also provide the early-stopping signal for
that fold model.   Consequently, those predictions are not fully independent of fold-level
model selection, and the procedure should not be described as completely leakage-free
OOFpredictiongeneration. Thesuppliedevidencedoesnotquantifyaresultingperformance
effect. Astricternestedprocedurecouldselectstoppingroundsusinganadditionalvalidation
subsetcontainedwithineachfold’strainingportion,thenpredicttheuntouchedfold;itwould
require implementation and a full rerun before being reported as a corrected result.

5.6.2   Validity Limitations and Research Implications

Dataset provenance and target semantics.The exact upstream release or archive corre-
sponding to the local CICIDS2017-associated CSV files has not been conclusively estab-
lished. Theauditnotesthattheavailableschemaincludesattributesconsistentwithversions
or improvements beyond the original basic flow representation; this is a provenance concern
requiring documentation, not proof of a particular source release. Until the archive, feature-
generation process, and label lineage are verified, results should be attributed to the study’s
processed CSV corpus rather than to a definitive canonical CICIDS2017 version.
    The current target policy preserves attack labels with the suffix-  Attempted; all labels
other than BENIGN are mapped to ATTACK for the binary task.  Whether an attempted-
attackannotationdenotesmaliciousactivityinthesameoperationalsenseastheotherlabels
dependsonthesourcedataset’sannotationsemantics. Thepresentevidencedoesnotestablish
thatthispolicyisnecessarilyincorrect,northateachnon-BENIGNannotationrepresentsan
independently verified attack behavior.  The label mapping therefore defines the evaluated
target construct and must be versioned and justified before the corrected experiments. This
distinctionmattersbecauseachangeintargetpolicychangesthelearningtask,classsupports,
and comparability of metrics.

Feature-vector overlap and split independence.The audit found 2,253 validation and
2,213testobservationswithexactmatchestotrainingobservationsacrossthe84model-input
features. The records are confirmed feature-vector matches, but the available evidence does
notproveidenticalsessionsorestablishthecauseofeachmatch. Nordoesitquantifytheeffect
onthereportedmetrics. Theoverlapneverthelessweakensaclaimthatallevaluationvectors
areuniquerelativetotrainingandmotivatesoverlap-awareand,wherefeasible,group-aware
partition analysis.  Exact duplicate-row removal does not by itself resolve this feature-level
overlap or dependence among related flows.   This is a threat to the independence of the
evaluation evidence, not a demonstrated numerical explanation for the near-perfect Stage 1
scores [4, 16].

LOACO scaling and OOF/calibration dependencies.The global StandardScaler was
fittedonthemaintrainingpartition,whichincludes66,600DDoStrainingobservationslater

are shared rather than refitted strictly inside each OOF fold.  These dependencies qualify
leakage-control claims; none has been shown to cause a known metric error of a particular
magnitude. Any corrected preprocessing, nested selection, or calibration procedure requires
new runs and separate result identifiers rather than reinterpretation of the current values.

Scope,classsupport,andcomparativeevidence.Thetestpartitionisrandomratherthan
established as chronological or grouped by session, host, or capture day.  The experiments
cover one processed benchmark corpus, one principal split, one documented random seed,
and a single held-out attack class.   There is no verified matched single-stage multiclass

possible flow clusters.

design,buttheycannotsupportclaimsthattheframeworkgeneralizestoallattackcategories,
is superior to flat classification, provides real-time efficiency, or detects operational zero-

classes,ashighlightedbyprioranalysisofCICIDS2017constructionandlabelling[4]. These
limitationsshouldqualifytheclaimsinChapter5withoutbeingmisrepresentedasproofthat
every existing result is invalid.
