## A. Abstract đề xuất

Network intrusion detection requires evaluation that distinguishes binary screening, attack classification, and recognition of categories absent from training. This thesis investigates an offline two-stage framework using a processed flow corpus associated with CICIDS2017. The corpus contains 2,096,804 observations, 84 numerical predictors, and 27 preserved labels. An Autoencoder produces a 16-dimensional representation. XGBoost and multilayer perceptron classifiers are compared for benign-versus-attack screening, while attack-only Stage-2 experiments examine whether appending the Stage-1 attack probability changes classification performance. Maximum-class-probability rejection, One-Class SVM, and Isolation Forest are evaluated in one leave-one-attack-class-out experiment holding out DDoS.

Under the existing exploratory protocol, standardized-feature XGBoost achieved a Stage-1 test F1 of 0.999857, compared with 0.999339 for its latent-feature counterpart. The latent-only Stage-2 XGBoost model had higher validation macro-F1 than the probability-augmented alternative, although their conditional test ordering was reversed. The latent-only composed pipeline achieved accuracy of 0.995298 and macro-F1 of 0.840758, with poor outcomes for some very low-support classes. For the DDoS holdout, maximum-probability rejection achieved standalone unknown recall of 0.260650 at a known-attack rejection rate of 0.049357; final pipeline unknown recall was 0.247197. Isolation Forest had higher standalone AUROC but lower recall at the selected operating point.

These findings do not establish a consistent benefit from latent compression or probability augmentation. Their interpretation is limited by unresolved dataset provenance, feature-vector overlap between partitions, shared-scaler exposure in the holdout experiment, and dependencies in out-of-fold model selection and calibration. The contribution is a traceable implementation and exploratory comparison with explicit analysis of component-to-system errors. Stronger claims require corrected protocols, matched baselines, and broader evaluation.

Keywords: network intrusion detection; Autoencoder; hierarchical classification; out-of-fold prediction; open-set evaluation.

## B. Thay phần thân §1.3 Research Objectives

The general objective of this research is to implement and evaluate an offline two-stage network intrusion detection framework, with attention to feature representation, binary screening, known-attack classification, and rejection of experimentally held-out attack categories.

The study pursues four specific objectives. First, it compares standardized original features with Autoencoder-derived latent features for Stage-1 detection. Second, it compares XGBoost and multilayer perceptron classifiers within each representation. Third, it examines the effect of adding a Stage-1 attack-probability feature to attack-only Stage-2 classification, using a within-XGBoost comparison to isolate the feature change in the available experiment matrix. Fourth, it evaluates three novelty mechanisms in the available DDoS holdout and distinguishes standalone novelty performance from final pipeline recognition.

The completed experiments provide exploratory evidence for these objectives under the implemented protocol. Dataset preparation, artifact traceability, and analysis of methodological limitations form part of the study. Corrections and additional experiments identified in the discussion remain future work and are not treated as completed evaluations.

## C. Thay phần thân §1.6 Research Methodology

The research follows an experimental machine-learning methodology. The processed corpus is partitioned into fixed training, validation, and test subsets. An Autoencoder learns a compact representation, and Stage-1 experiments compare two classifier families using standardized and latent inputs. Stage 2 is trained on known attack categories and is evaluated both conditionally on true attack observations and within the composed system.

The probability-feature experiments use five-fold Stage-1 predictions to construct training scores and apply a Platt-style mapping before appending the resulting scalar to the latent vector. The current implementation has model-selection and calibration dependencies: the nominally held-out fold also supplies early-stopping information, and the calibrator is fitted to and reapplied to the same OOF training scores. Consequently, these features are described as generated by the implemented OOF procedure, rather than as fully independent cross-fitted estimates.

Unknown-category behavior is assessed in one DDoS holdout using maximum-probability rejection, One-Class SVM, and Isolation Forest. Novelty thresholds are selected from known-attack validation scores. The shared scaler was fitted before holdout-specific exclusion, which limits strict unknown-class isolation. Evaluation therefore separates saved exploratory findings from the corrective procedures proposed for future experiments.

Performance is reported using task-appropriate aggregate, class-aware, and unknown-rejection measures. The analysis distinguishes ranking metrics from operating-point decisions and component results from final composed labels. Computational advantages are not inferred from dimensionality reduction because a complete pipeline timing benchmark is unavailable.

Giữ các citation về stacking, calibration và novelty của phần tương ứng: `wolpert1992stacked`, `platt2000probabilities`, `guo2017calibration`, `scholkopf2001support`, `liu2008isolation`. Không cần đưa mọi nguồn vào cùng một câu.

## D. Đổi §1.7 thành Research Significance and Contributions

This thesis contributes an implemented, artifact-traceable framework for studying the relationship between binary screening, attack classification, and unknown-category rejection. Its empirical contribution is an exploratory comparison of the specified model and representation configurations, together with a probability-feature ablation and analysis of errors introduced at different stages of the composed decision.

The results provide evidence that is useful even where the proposed components do not improve the selected metrics. In particular, the current experiments do not demonstrate a consistent advantage from latent compression or probability augmentation. The distinction between standalone novelty scoring and final unknown recognition also reveals why evaluating a detector separately is insufficient to characterize the complete system.

These contributions concern implementation and bounded empirical analysis. They do not establish a new underlying learning algorithm, state-of-the-art performance, general detection of operational zero-day exploits, or deployment readiness. The methodological audit identifies the conditions that must be addressed before stronger comparative claims can be supported.

## G. Thay §4.2.3 Class Distribution and Imbalance và Bảng 4.6

The processed corpus contains 27 preserved labels. Summing the class-by-split counts in `results/split_summary.csv` yields 2,096,804 observations: 1,467,762 training, 314,521 validation, and 314,521 test observations. BENIGN accounts for 1,582,535 observations, or approximately 75.47% of the corpus. The remaining 514,269 observations belong to the 26 preserved attack categories.

The class distribution is highly uneven. The smallest category, Web Attack - SQL Injection - Attempted, contains five observations, allocated as three training, one validation, and one test observation. Infiltration has five test observations, and Web Attack - SQL Injection has two. Such supports make individual class estimates sensitive to very small numbers of errors. Macro-averaged measures complement pooled measures but do not eliminate this sampling uncertainty.

The complete class-by-split counts are reported in the accompanying distribution table. These counts describe the current target mapping and exploratory partition; they are not universal class totals for every CICIDS2017 release. The separate file `results/dataset_summary.csv` records source-file row counts, column counts, sizes, and checksums, rather than label frequencies.

## H. Bổ sung §4.2.4: quy tắc lớp hiếm

The implementation assigns samples separately within each label after a seeded random permutation. For classes with at least three observations, validation and test counts are obtained by rounding their nominal proportions, with a minimum of one observation in each; if those allocations would exhaust the class, both are set to one and the remainder is used for training. A class with one observation is assigned to training, while a class with two observations is divided between training and test. These rules preserve representation where possible, but they do not create reliable class-level estimates when support is very small.

Nguồn đã đọc trực tiếp: `src/split_data.py::constrained_stratified_split()`. Đây là mô tả quy tắc hiện tại; không phải đề xuất tăng số mẫu. Sau này đổi split phải cập nhật đoạn này và bảng support.

## I. Thay phần cuối §4.4.4 về final unknown recall

The JSON field `open_set_metrics` summarizes novelty scores on true attack test observations and does not itself measure final unknown recognition after Stage-1 gating. Final-label unknown precision, recall, and F1 must instead be calculated from the composed predictions against the open-set ground truth. The internal audit performed this calculation for the saved MCP LOACO-26 prediction file; the resulting values are reported separately in Section 5.5.2. They should not be attributed to an identically defined field in the original pipeline JSON.

## J. Chương 6 đề xuất: Conclusions and Future Work

### 6.1 Conclusions

This thesis investigated an offline two-stage intrusion detection framework using a processed flow corpus associated with CICIDS2017. The study combined an Autoencoder-derived representation, binary benign-versus-attack screening, attack-only classification, and optional unknown-category rejection. The existing experiments were analyzed as exploratory evidence under the implemented protocol, with separate attention to component performance and final system decisions.

**RQ1: Effect of the learned representation.** The current Stage-1 comparisons did not show a binary F1 improvement from replacing the 84 standardized predictors with the 16-dimensional latent vector. XGBoost achieved test F1 of 0.999857 with standardized inputs and 0.999339 with latent inputs; the standardized MLP configuration also exceeded its latent counterpart. These findings concern the tested configurations and fixed partition. They do not establish that learned representations are generally ineffective, and the reduced input dimension does not demonstrate lower total inference cost without measurement.

**RQ2: Comparison of Stage-1 classifiers.** XGBoost had higher observed binary test F1 than the MLP within both tested representations. This is a numerical comparison of the implemented configurations, rather than a universal ranking of classifier families. The composed latent-based pipelines used the XGBoost latent probability stream. That choice identifies the evaluated architecture and should not be interpreted as evidence that it was the highest-validation-F1 Stage-1 configuration across the entire comparison matrix.

**RQ3: Effect of the attack-probability feature.** The available within-family ablation compared latent-only XGBoost with XGBoost using latent features and the Stage-1 probability. Validation macro-F1 favored latent-only inputs, whereas conditional known-attack test macro-F1 favored augmentation. Final closed-set macro-F1 was 0.840758 for the latent-only pipeline and 0.837458 for the probability-augmented XGBoost pipeline. The observations therefore do not demonstrate a consistent benefit across validation, conditional classification, and final system evaluation. The absence of a latent-only MLP baseline also prevents isolating the feature effect within that family.

**RQ4: Recognition of a held-out attack category.** The DDoS LOACO-26 experiment provided standalone results for maximum-probability rejection, One-Class SVM, and Isolation Forest. At the validation-selected operating point, maximum-probability rejection achieved unknown recall of 0.260650 with known-attack rejection of 0.049357. Isolation Forest had the highest AUROC but lower recall at its selected threshold, demonstrating the distinction between ranking quality and operating-point performance. The saved MCP composed pipeline achieved final unknown recall of 0.247197. This final measure includes the effect of the Stage-1 gate and is distinct from attack-only score evaluation. One held-out benchmark category does not establish general recognition of unknown attack families or operational zero-day exploits.

The system-level analysis also showed why overall accuracy is insufficient. The latent-only closed-set pipeline achieved accuracy of 0.995298 and weighted-F1 of 0.995308, while its macro-F1 was 0.840758 and some rare categories had final F1 equal to zero. Their very small supports limit the stability of class-level estimates. Reporting aggregate performance alongside support and error pathways is therefore essential to interpreting this framework.

### 6.2 Contributions and Limitations

The study's present contribution is a traceable implementation and exploratory evaluation of the specified components, including a within-XGBoost probability-feature comparison and analysis of errors between component and composed predictions. The results do not establish an original learning algorithm, superiority over an unevaluated flat classifier, or readiness for live deployment. Negative and mixed findings remain informative because they identify which benefits are not supported by the available evidence.

Several limitations constrain interpretation. The exact upstream dataset release and the semantics of the preserved attempted-attack labels require further documentation. Input-vector matches across partitions weaken assumptions of independent evaluation observations. The shared scaler was exposed to the category later held out in LOACO, so that experiment does not satisfy strict preprocessing isolation. The OOF procedure also has early-stopping and calibration dependencies. Finally, the evidence concerns one principal split, one documented seed, one held-out attack class, and incomplete matched baselines; no complete pipeline latency benchmark or independent external evaluation is available. The magnitude and direction of any performance change after correcting these conditions have not been established.

### 6.3 Future Work

The first priority is to establish dataset and target provenance, then define versioned partitions and preprocessing rules consistent with the intended inference question. For strict LOACO, every fitted transformation and model must exclude the held-out category, with thresholds determined from permitted known validation observations. Probability-feature generation should document independent stopping and calibration decisions at the relevant fold level, together with evaluation of probability reliability.

Subsequent experiments should add the matched baselines required by the retained claims, including a flat multiclass comparator if the benefit of the cascade is investigated and a latent-only MLP if within-MLP feature effects are studied. Broader unknown-category claims require multiple eligible holdouts, and robustness claims require suitable repeated or independent evaluations. Resource comparisons should include measured preprocessing, encoding, classification, and rejection costs under a controlled timing protocol.

New results must retain their own configurations, provenance, and run identifiers. Because the existing test outcomes have already been inspected, a new seed or output directory alone does not establish an independent confirmatory test population. The present exploratory findings should remain archived and be distinguished from subsequent corrected experiments. The defensible outcome of this thesis is evidence about the evaluated configurations and their limitations, rather than a presumption that combining the components guarantees improved detection.
